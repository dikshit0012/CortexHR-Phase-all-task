## 1. Is headcount increasing or decreasing?

Increasing. Company headcount went from 171 (Jan 2020) to 441 (Sep 2026); a straight-line fit adds 3.84 people per month. It rose in 74% of months, fell in 19% and was unchanged in 8%. Calendar-year growth for the 6 complete years: 2020: year-end 172; 2021: +31 (+18.0%); 2022: +67 (+33.0%); 2023: +66 (+24.4%); 2024: +42 (+12.5%); 2025: +20 (+5.3%). Year-over-year growth ranged from 2.9% to 33.0% and was 11.9% in the latest month.

## 2. Are there seasonal patterns?

Weak-to-moderate evidence. Average net change by calendar month is highest in Jun, Jan, Jul (+6.1, +6.0, +5.7 people) and lowest in Apr, May (+1.0, -0.6). A Kruskal-Wallis test on net change by calendar month gives p = 0.024; for hires alone p = 0.129 and for exits alone p = 0.618. Each calendar month has only 6-7 observations, so the pattern is statistically suggestive, not firmly established; it is tested against a non-seasonal model in the backtest rather than assumed.

## 3. Which months show unusual changes?

The weakest stretch is May 2020 (headcount 162), inside the COVID-19 hiring freeze event (EV-01). The latest month is itself unusual if listed above, which matters for the starting point of the forecast.

## 4. Are there structural changes in workforce size?

Annual net change in people: 2021: +31; 2022: +67; 2023: +66; 2024: +42; 2025: +20; 2026 so far (9 months): +43. Growth in absolute terms therefore slowed after its peak and re-accelerated in 2026 (already +43 versus +20 in the last full year). A simple comparison of mean monthly net change in 2020-2022 (+2.83) and 2023 onward (+3.80) gives Welch p = 0.32, so a single permanent level shift is not supported; the pattern is better described as episodes of growth than as one structural break. During the 3 freeze months the average net change was -1.67; during the 13 months touched by a company-relevant hiring-drive event it was +4.31, against +3.42 in all other months (descriptive, not causal).

## 5. Which periods have insufficient historical information?

* 2020 has no year-over-year comparison (first 12 months) and contains the COVID freeze.
* Each calendar month appears only 6-7 times, which limits seasonal estimates.
* Rolling-origin validation needs 36 months of training, so only the later 45 months can be used as forecast tests.
* Small departments (e.g. D00 with one person; D10 and D05 in the low teens) have too few people for stable percentage-based statements.
* There is no information about future events: the workforce_events sheet ends in April 2026 and lists no planned events.
